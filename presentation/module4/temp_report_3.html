4. Synthetic Experiments and Results

4.1 Experiment E2: Forward Solution Verification

With baseline parameters β = 0.4858 day−1 (R0 = 2.0), μ = 0.1000 day−1, and initial fractions (0.99, 0.005, 0.005, 0, 0), the PINN was trained purely on physics loss Lode in the absence of observation data. Across 3 independent seeds, mean relative L2 errors against high-order DOP853 integration were 3.7×10−4 (s), 5.4×10−4 (e), 5.1×10−4 (i), 1.1×10−3 (r), and 7.9×10−4 (d). The maximum pointwise error across all compartments remained under 2.0×10−3, with conservation maintained at 2×10−16.

A synthetic epidemic incorporating an intervention step reducing β from 0.55 to 0.25 at day 30 was observed through noisy cumulative counts (1% and 5% multiplicative noise). The PINN reconstructed β(t), μ, initial states, and unobserved trajectories s(t), e(t), i(t).

5. Conclusion and Critical Discussion

The SEIRD-PINN successfully resolves the epidemiological observability paradox: from noisy, aggregated reports of cumulative cases and deaths alone, it recovers continuous non-monotonic transmission trajectories β(t), the case-fatality parameter μ, and latent infection compartments. The ablation confirms that second-order L-BFGS refinement and relative residual scaling are mathematically essential for convergence.
